Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted a comprehensive audit of the Dopex rDPX V2 protocol, focusing on its core contracts, perpetual vaults, liquidity-management components, oracle integrations, pricing libraries, and external exchange integrations.

The review covered the system architecture, including component relationships, asset and funding flows, option purchase and settlement operations, oracle-driven pricing, LP accounting, reserve management, decaying bonds, ReLP operations, and Uniswap and StableSwap liquidity integrations.

Our team confirmed two high-impact issues and one medium-impact issue. The findings include an exploitable deposit-ordering flaw that can misprice LP shares when accrued funding is recognized after share calculation, and a Uniswap V3 AMO design that permits caller-controlled slippage and adverse MEV execution. We also identified a high-confidence risk that stale or manipulable last-price data could affect option and LP valuation. Overall, the protocol contains meaningful administrative and reentrancy protections, but accounting synchronization, oracle integrity, and swap execution controls require remediation before the system can be considered robust against financial-manipulation scenarios.

System Overview

The Hound team analyzed Dopex rDPX V2 as a protocol centered on RdpxV2Core, which coordinates perpetual vault operations, reserves, decaying bonds, dpxETH issuance, liquidity managers, and external market integrations. RdpxV2Core interacts with IPerpetualAtlanticVault, IRdpxReserve, IRdpxDecayingBonds, IDpxEthToken, and related interfaces to manage collateral, funding, token minting, reserve transfers, and protocol control paths.

The PerpetualAtlanticVault provides option purchase and settlement functionality and depends on IOptionPricing, IVolatilityOracle, and asset-price data supplied through oracle interfaces including IRdpxEthOracle and IDpxEthOracle. OptionPricingSimple uses BlackScholes and consumes volatility and asset-price inputs. DpxEthOracle publishes dpxETH-related pricing data and applies heartbeat-related validation, but the architecture still exposes consumers to risks associated with fresh yet manipulated prices or inconsistent freshness enforcement across pricing paths.

PerpetualAtlanticVaultLP maintains LP collateral and share accounting, including rDPX collateral, deposits, withdrawals, funding updates, and settlement-related asset changes. Our analysis revealed that deposit share calculations can occur before PerpetualAtlanticVault.updateFunding() recognizes accrued funding, allowing a depositor to receive shares using a lower pre-funding collateral value and subsequently benefit from the increased collateral balance. Settlement paths perform multiple operations in a single non-reentrant transaction, but all balance-affecting paths should maintain atomic synchronization between token transfers and recorded collateral.

The protocol also includes ReLPContract and the UniV2LiquidityAmo and UniV3LiquidityAmo components, which move assets between RdpxV2Core and external liquidity venues. The AMOs use Uniswap router, pair, pool, factory, and position-manager interfaces, while RdpxV2Core additionally integrates with StableSwap and Curve factory interfaces. We discovered that UniV3LiquidityAmo forwards caller-supplied amountOutMinimum and sqrtPriceLimitX96 values without enforcing a protocol-level quote, bounded slippage tolerance, or transaction-specific deadline, allowing adverse execution and sandwich exposure when administrators submit permissive parameters.

The Hound team identified oracle, accounting, and execution controls as the principal security boundaries. The confirmed findings were reviewed by openai-codex/gpt-5.6-luna. Recommended improvements include deriving swap limits from validated oracle or TWAP prices, enforcing consistent price snapshots and freshness checks, making funding recognition occur before deposit pricing, and deriving or reconciling managed collateral from verified token balances wherever practical.

Vault-only collateral updates can leave LP accounting stale across settlement and liquidity flows

This accounting error arises because collateral state is updated through a restricted vault-only pathway rather than being derived from, or atomically synchronized with, all token transfers and settlement events. The option settlement flow can transfer assets to LP-held balances without completing the corresponding collateral update, while LP share valuation continues to include rDPX and other managed assets based on potentially stale accounting data.

An attacker could monitor settlement, funding, or other balance-changing operations and deposit or withdraw LP shares before the accounting state is synchronized. By interacting during this window, the attacker may obtain shares at an outdated exchange rate or redeem shares for more assets than warranted, causing losses to other liquidity providers and distorting vault solvency and share pricing. The issue is particularly material where settlement transfers and collateral updates are executed in separate transactions or through non-atomic call paths.

Affected Components: the Perpetual Atlantic Vault LP component, the Perpetual Atlantic Vault component, and the option settlement flow, specifically collateral accounting, LP share valuation, settlement, and LP update operations

QA Review: Upon reviewing the deposit and funding paths in PerpetualAtlanticVaultLP and PerpetualAtlanticVault, I confirmed an exploitable accounting-ordering flaw. PerpetualAtlanticVaultLP.deposit() calculates shares through previewDeposit() before calling perpetualAtlanticVault.updateFunding(). previewDeposit() uses totalCollateral() and _rdpxCollateral to determine the exchange rate, while updateFunding() may subsequently transfer accrued funding into the LP and call addProceeds(), increasing _totalCollateral. A depositor can therefore submit a deposit immediately before accrued funding is recognized and receive shares calculated against the lower pre-funding collateral value; after funding is added, those shares represent more than the depositor’s fair portion and dilute existing LP holders. The redeem() path is safer because it calls updateFunding() before redeemPreview(). Settlement itself is largely atomic: settle() calls updateFunding(), performs transfers, and then updates LP accounting in the same nonReentrant transaction, so it does not create a persistent cross-transaction stale window. Nevertheless, the deposit ordering provides a concrete and exploitable accounting vulnerability consistent with the hypothesis.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Make balance-affecting operations and collateral-account updates atomic, derive managed assets from verified token balances where practical, and block deposits and withdrawals whenever the accounting state is stale or an update is pending.

Caller-controlled Uniswap V3 minimum output exposes vault swaps to adverse execution

This slippage and MEV vulnerability results from allowing the caller to supply the minimum acceptable swap output without enforcing a protocol-level slippage bound, validated oracle price, or transaction-specific quote. The UniV3 AMO can therefore execute administrator-authorized swaps using a minimum-output value that may be materially below the fair market value, with insufficient validation of the deadline, pool parameters, or realized execution price.

An MEV searcher could sandwich an authorized swap by moving the Uniswap V3 pool price before execution and trading in the opposite direction afterward. If the caller-selected minimum output is permissive, the swap would still succeed at an unfavorable rate, allowing the searcher to capture the resulting value difference. Repeated or large adverse executions could deplete assets held by the liquidity-management flow and impose direct losses on the protocol.

Affected Components: the UniV3 AMO component, the Uniswap V3 router interface, and the rDPX v2 core component, specifically swap execution and liquidity-manager asset transfers

QA Review: Upon reviewing UniV3LiquidityAMO.sol, specifically the admin-only swap function, I confirmed the slippage/MEV vulnerability exists. The function accepts _amountOutMinimum and _sqrtPriceLimitX96 directly from the authorized caller, transfers tokens from rdpxV2Core, and forwards both values unchanged to ISwapRouter.exactInputSingle through ExactInputSingleParams. There is no protocol-enforced minimum derived from an oracle, TWAP, reference quote, or configured slippageTolerance, and zero is not rejected as a minimum output. The deadline is hardcoded to 2105300114 rather than being transaction-specific, providing an unusually long execution window. Although only DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE can invoke the function, an authorized administrator or automation system can submit a transaction with a permissive minimum output, allowing an MEV searcher to sandwich the swap and cause adverse execution while still satisfying the supplied bound. The administrator trust boundary prevents unauthorized direct invocation but does not mitigate the stated execution-risk vulnerability.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Compute and enforce minimum output from a bounded oracle/TWAP quote and configured maximum slippage, require deadline and pool-parameter validation, and reject swaps whose realized price deviates beyond the protocol limit.

Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Revert Lend protocol, focusing on its lending vault, collateral valuation, debt accounting, liquidation flows, interest-rate calculations, and position-management integrations.

The review covered the system architecture and interaction flow between lenders, borrowers, V3Vault, V3Oracle, InterestRateModel, FlashloanLiquidator, Swapper, leverage transformers, automators, Uniswap V3 position infrastructure, and external pricing and token dependencies.

Our team confirmed one high-severity security issue involving spot-price manipulation of concentrated liquidity position collateral. V3Oracle derives LP token amounts from the live Uniswap V3 spot price and validates that price against another spot-based reference, allowing temporary price movements within the configured tolerance to affect collateral valuation and borrowing health checks. The overall security posture depends materially on oracle configuration, pool liquidity, position ranges, and conservative risk parameters; independent TWAP validation and valuation haircuts are recommended before production reliance.

System Overview

The core lending boundary is V3Vault, which implements IVault and manages ERC-4626 lending shares, borrower debt shares, NFT collateral, deposits, withdrawals, borrowing, repayment, liquidation, and position transformations. Borrowing and repayment update loan accounting, while health checks determine whether a position can incur additional debt or remain eligible for liquidation. V3Vault also depends on InterestRateModel for rate calculations and on V3Oracle for collateral valuation.

V3Oracle evaluates Uniswap V3 position NFTs by obtaining pool state, calculating the position's token amounts, and applying configured token pricing sources and feed settings. The oracle exposes getValue and getPositionBreakdown, while token configuration and secondary price validation provide additional safeguards. Our analysis revealed that the position composition is calculated from the current pool spot price, and the secondary pool-price comparison also uses a spot observation rather than an independent manipulation-resistant TWAP.

The principal lending risk is therefore concentrated in the relationship between V3Oracle and V3Vault collateral checks. A temporary pool-price movement within maxPoolPriceDifference can alter the calculated composition and reported value of a concentrated LP position without causing a revert. V3Vault.borrow then relies on that valuation when checking loan health, so a borrower may increase debt against an inflated temporary valuation and restore the market price afterward. The same valuation path can also influence loan information and liquidation decisions.

Liquidation can be performed directly through V3Vault or through FlashloanLiquidator. The flash-loan flow obtains loan information, invokes the Uniswap V3 flash callback, swaps assets through Swapper, and routes the resulting liquidation through V3Vault. LeverageTransformer similarly borrows from the vault, swaps assets, and increases liquidity, while V3Utils handles balance-delta accounting and NFT-related operations. These flows create important trust boundaries around token transfers, router behavior, callback authorization, and external pool execution.

Automator components including AutoCompound, AutoRange, and AutoExit operate on externally owned or vault-held Uniswap V3 positions through approved transformation paths. They rely on Automator authorization, Swapper routing, pool-price checks, operator permissions, and residual-balance accounting. The Hound team identified the oracle's spot-price dependence as the principal confirmed issue in the reviewed architecture; strengthening valuation with independent TWAP observations, strict deviation limits, conservative haircuts, and pause controls would reduce the risk of bad debt and delayed liquidation.

Spot-price manipulation of concentrated LP collateral passes oracle validation

This is a price-manipulation and oracle-validation logic error in the valuation of concentrated-liquidity positions. The V3Oracle derives the position's token composition and collateral value from the pool's current spot price. Its anti-manipulation check compares the oracle-derived price with that same potentially manipulated spot price and permits a configurable deviation, so the check does not provide an independent validation of the valuation input. For narrow-range positions, even a price movement within the permitted threshold can materially inflate the reported collateral value.

An attacker could use a flash swap or temporary liquidity to move the Uniswap V3 pool price toward a favorable tick, keep the movement below maxPoolPriceDifference, and then borrow against the inflated position valuation. The pool price could be restored within the same transaction or before the next block, leaving the vault with undercollateralized debt. Depending on the configured deviation limit, pool liquidity, position range, and collateral parameters, this could result in excessive borrowing, inaccurate loan health calculations, and losses during liquidation.

Affected Components: the V3Oracle contract, specifically getValue(), _checkPoolPrice(), and _getAmounts(), and the V3Vault contract, specifically _checkLoanIsHealthy(), borrow(), and loanInfo(); the issue also affects the vault’s collateral checks and liquidation logic

QA Review: Upon reviewing V3Oracle.getValue, V3Oracle._getAmounts, V3Oracle._checkPoolPrice, and V3Vault._checkLoanIsHealthy/borrow, I confirmed that the hypothesized spot-price manipulation path exists. In V3Oracle._initializeState and _getAmounts, the position’s token amounts are calculated from pool.slot0().sqrtPriceX96, making collateral composition dependent on the current Uniswap V3 spot price. Although getValue obtains token prices from configured Chainlink/TWAP sources, its anti-manipulation check derives an oracle pool price from those same prices and compares it against _getReferencePoolPriceX96(pool, 0), which explicitly reads the manipulable current slot0 price rather than a TWAP. _requireMaxDifference only rejects deviations at or above the configurable maxPoolPriceDifference, with a minimum configuration of 2%, so a temporary price movement below the threshold is accepted while still changing the LP amounts and resulting collateral valuation. V3Vault.borrow invokes _requireLoanIsHealthy using this valuation, allowing debt to be increased during the manipulated state. Restoring the pool price afterward does not undo the debt, and no independent spot/TWAP guard or valuation haircut prevents this path.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Value LP amounts using a sufficiently long, manipulation-resistant TWAP or an independently observed position price, and validate the position pool's price against an independent TWAP before using it for borrowing or liquidation. Add conservative valuation haircuts and reject or pause valuation when spot/TWAP deviation exceeds a strict bound; do not use the same spot observation as both the valuation input and verification source.

Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the WOOFi Protocol, reviewing its user-facing swap, deposit, vault, lending, reward, fee-management, oracle, and cross-chain functionality.

The review covered the system architecture, including component relationships, asset and control flows, pricing and oracle dependencies, vault and strategy interactions, administrative authorization, reward distribution, and external bridge, token, DEX, and messaging boundaries.

Our team confirmed a high-severity vault share-price manipulation issue in WOOFiVaultV2. Direct token donations can inflate the vault's raw asset balance and reduce the shares minted to later depositors, potentially allowing an attacker to extract value from victim deposits. The protocol otherwise presents a broad, modular architecture with meaningful access controls and reentrancy protections, but vault accounting and user-facing slippage protections require remediation before the affected functionality can be considered robust.

System Overview

The WOOFi Protocol is organized around WooRouterV2 and WooRouterV3, which receive user swap requests and route token transfers into WooPPV2 and WooPPV3. The pricing-pool components rely on oracle data from WooracleV2 and related deployments, while WooPPV3 additionally uses WooPPBase and interacts with WooLendingManager. WooracleV3 obtains pricing information through an AggregatorV3Interface, creating an external data boundary that is critical to swap pricing and pool solvency.

Administrative and economic controls are divided among WooAccessManager, WooFeeManager, WooRebateManager, and WooVaultManager. WooAccessManager authorizes privileged fee-management operations, while WooFeeManager distributes protocol fees to rebate and vault-management components. WooVaultManager controls interactions with WOOFiVaultV2, which holds user assets and delegates activity to strategy contracts such as BaseStrategy and VoidStrategy. The vault also interacts with WETH-related token functionality and uses non-reentrancy and transfer validation mechanisms in relevant asset flows.

Our analysis revealed that the vault's share issuance and redemption depend on the vault's current raw want-token balance. Because that balance can be increased through unsolicited token transfers without increasing total share supply, an attacker can manipulate the exchange rate through a direct donation before a victim deposit. The absence of virtual assets or shares, internally maintained asset accounting, and user-supplied minimum-share or maximum-price constraints leaves the deposit path exposed to donation-based dilution and potential extraction of victim funds.

The protocol also includes WooSuperChargerVaultV2 and WooLendingManager variants for lending and performance-fee flows, together with MasterChefWoo, WooSimpleRewarder, and DataProvider for reward accounting and reporting. Cross-chain functionality is implemented through WooCrossChainRouterV2, WooCrossChainRouterV3, WooCrossChainRouterV4, WooUsdOFTCrossRouter, WOOFiDexCrossChainRouter, and WooCrossRouterForWidget. These components call WooRouterV2, use nonce and Stargate-related interfaces, and bridge assets through WooUsdOFT or external messaging infrastructure.

Security boundaries therefore include privileged administrators, oracle and aggregator inputs, ERC-20 and WETH token behavior, strategy and lending callbacks, Stargate routing, OFT transfers, and widget nonce authorization. The Hound team identified these boundaries as requiring strict validation of callers, message origin and destination, token accounting, fee calculations, oracle freshness, and cross-chain replay resistance. Remediation of the WOOFiVaultV2 accounting weakness should prioritize virtual liquidity or equivalent inflation-attack defenses and explicit deposit slippage controls.

Unsolicited asset donation can manipulate vault share price and dilute deposits

This share-accounting logic error allows unsolicited token donations to manipulate the vault's share price and dilute subsequent deposits. The WOOFiVaultV2 deposit logic calculates newly issued shares using the vault's raw want-token balance and existing total share supply. Because the balance can be increased through direct token transfers that do not mint shares, the implementation lacks virtual assets or shares, internal asset accounting, and user-provided minimum-share or price-slippage protections.

Affected Components: the WOOFiVaultV2 contract, specifically the deposit(), balance(), and withdraw() functions

QA Review: Upon reviewing WOOFiVaultV2.deposit(), balance(), and withdraw() in VaultV2.sol, I confirmed that the vault is vulnerable to donation-based share-price manipulation. deposit() records balanceBefore using balance(), which includes the vault's raw want-token balance returned by available() and any strategy balance. An attacker can therefore transfer want tokens directly to the vault, increasing balanceBefore without receiving shares. For an existing nonzero totalSupply, shares are then calculated as (amount * totalSupply()) / balanceBefore, with only a shares > 0 check and no minimum-share or slippage parameter. A sufficiently large donation can cause a victim's deposit to mint disproportionately few shares or revert at the zero-share check; with rounding, it can mint zero shares while the victim's assets remain in the vault. withdraw() calculates redemption from the manipulated balance and totalSupply, allowing the attacker to redeem their shares for assets that include the victim's deposit. The nonReentrant guard and amount-transfer validation do not prevent unsolicited transfers or protect the exchange rate, and no virtual liquidity or internal asset accounting is implemented.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Use virtual assets and virtual shares or another ERC-4626 inflation-attack defense; alternatively maintain internally accounted assets rather than using the raw token balance, and expose deposit minimum-shares/max-price parameters so users can reject manipulated issuance rates.

Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Spartan Protocol, reviewing its core contracts, token flows, liquidity mechanisms, vaults, registries, and governance-controlled configuration paths.

The review covered the System Architecture, including user-facing Router, DAO, SynthVault, SynthFactory, and PoolFactory entrypoints; Pool and Synth interactions; BASE, BNB, WBNB, and external token flows; deployment and registry relationships; and DAO control over protocol parameters, reserves, and vault operations.

Our team confirmed three material security issues: manipulation of spot-price-based SynthVault weights and rewards, manipulation of reserve-funded pool dividends, and missing user slippage protections on Router swap and synth-issuance routes. We also identified a conditional high-severity risk from permissionless pool initialization with attacker-selected pricing. The protocol's primary risks arise from reliance on mutable AMM spot prices and insufficient transaction-level execution safeguards.

System Overview

The Spartan architecture centers on Router, PoolFactory, Pool, SynthFactory, Synth, DAO, Utils, Reserve, and the DAO, Bond, and Synth vaults. PoolFactory deploys pools and records token-to-pool and curated-pool relationships, while SynthFactory deploys synthetic assets and maintains token-to-synth registries. Router resolves pools through these registries and provides user-facing liquidity, swap, synth conversion, and withdrawal flows.

Liquidity and trading flows use BASE and external BEP20 assets, with Router also handling BNB and WBNB conversion paths. Pools maintain BASE and token reserves, calculate swap outputs from current reserves, transfer assets, and mint or burn Synth tokens for supported issuance and redemption operations. Utils provides valuation and pricing helpers used by Pool, SynthVault, DAO, and other components, making its reserve-ratio calculations security-sensitive.

The DAO is the principal governance and configuration authority. It configures Router, Utils, Reserve, and PoolFactory, authorizes pool creation, coordinates vault operations, and interacts with Reserve-funded emissions and protocol controls. DaoVault manages LP deposits and withdrawals with access restrictions and a withdrawal lock, BondVault manages DAO-gated claims and vesting, and SynthVault accepts deposits, records valuation-derived weights, and distributes reserve-funded rewards.

The Hound team identified that SynthVault persists deposit weights derived from instantaneous Pool reserve ratios. Because permissionless swaps can temporarily distort those ratios, an attacker can deposit during a manipulated pricing window and retain an inflated reward weight after restoring the pool price. We also discovered that swap fees used for reserve-funded dividends can be converted using mutable post-trade spot prices, potentially causing oversized Reserve grants to manipulated pools.

Our analysis revealed that Router swap and synth routes do not accept minimum-output, maximum-input, deadline, or equivalent price-limit parameters, leaving users exposed to sandwich attacks and unfavorable execution. In addition, permissionless pool initialization allows arbitrary starting reserve ratios with limited validation; if such a pool becomes curated or otherwise used by price-sensitive flows, its attacker-selected initial price could affect synthetic issuance and valuation. TWAP or independent oracle checks, deviation limits, minimum liquidity requirements, initialization controls, and explicit user slippage protections would materially improve the protocol's security posture.

Manipulable pool spot price overweights SynthVault deposits and rewards

This is a high-severity oracle-manipulation logic error in vault valuation and reward accounting. The SynthVault records a deposit weight using the current AMM reserve ratio through calcSpotValueInBase() and calcSpotValueInBaseWithPool(). Because the ratio is derived from a mutable pool spot price without a time-weighted average price, external reference, bounded deviation check, or valuation snapshot, the recorded weight can be materially overstated at the time of deposit and remains persistent after the pool price normalizes.

An attacker can use swaps to inflate the curated pool’s spot valuation, deposit synths acquired in advance while the valuation is distorted, and then restore the pool price. After the deposit lockout expires, the attacker can harvest rewards calculated from the inflated weight, causing reserve-funded rewards to be misallocated and potentially diluting legitimate depositors. The risk is greatest in pools with limited liquidity, where relatively small trades can produce significant price movements.

Affected Components: the Pool contract, the Utils library, and the SynthVault contract, specifically calcSpotValueInBase(), calcSpotValueInBaseWithPool(), _deposit(), harvestSingle(), and calcReward(), as well as the user synth deposit flow

QA Review: Upon reviewing SynthVault._deposit and Utils.calcSpotValueInBase/calcSpotValueInBaseWithPool, I confirmed that vault weight is calculated from the live pool reserve ratio and permanently stored. The pricing function directly returns amount * pool.baseAmount() / pool.tokenAmount(), while Pool.swap and swapTo are permissionless and update these reserves, allowing an attacker to temporarily increase the apparent base value of a synth. The attacker can then deposit pre-acquired synths during the manipulated state, causing mapMemberSynth_weight, mapMemberTotal_weight, and totalWeight to record an inflated value. After restoring the pool ratio, calcReward and calcCurrentReward continue using the persisted inflated weight to determine the attacker’s share of reserve-funded emissions. harvestSingle additionally values newly minted synth rewards using the same mutable spot price. The one-hour lockout only delays withdrawal and harvesting; it does not validate the recorded valuation or bind it to an external reference, TWAP, deviation limit, or snapshot. Therefore, the manipulation can persist beyond price restoration and increase reward extraction relative to honest depositors.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Use a robust external or TWAP oracle with stale-price and deviation checks for vault valuation; alternatively defer weight calculation until a manipulation-resistant accounting window and cap per-user weight changes and reward accrual.

high

Spot-price manipulation can amplify reserve-funded curated-pool dividends

This is a high-severity economic and oracle-manipulation logic error in curated-pool dividend allocation. The router converts swap fees into a base-value amount using the instantaneous spot ratio of the same AMM pool. That ratio can be changed through permissionless trades and is not anchored to an independent reference price, a time-weighted average, or a cap based on actual fees or executed volume.

An attacker can first trade to distort the pool reserves and the resulting baseAmount-to-tokenAmount ratio, execute a swap whose fee is valued at the manipulated price, and trigger dividend processing. The reserve may then grant an inflated dividend to the curated pool, with the attacker potentially recovering part of the grant through LP ownership or subsequent pool interactions. The impact is particularly significant for low-liquidity pools, although profitability depends on liquidity-provider ownership and the cost of manipulating and reversing the price.

Affected Components: the Pool contract, the Utils library, and the Router contract, specifically _swapBaseToToken(), _swapTokenToBase(), mintSynth(), calcSpotValueInBase(), calcSpotValueInBaseWithPool(), getsDividend(), and addDividend(), together with the Reserve-funded dividend flow

QA Review: Upon reviewing Pool._swapBaseToToken, Pool.mintSynth, Utils.calcSpotValueInBaseWithPool, and Router.getsDividend/addDividend, I confirmed that reserve-funded dividends can be influenced by a permissionless trade. In _swapBaseToToken, the fee is first calculated in token units using the pre-trade reserves, but the pool reserves are then updated through _setPoolAmounts before calcSpotValueInBase(TOKEN, fee) converts that fee into BASE units. Consequently, the conversion uses the post-trade spot ratio, which the same trader can materially distort, particularly in a low-liquidity pool. mintSynth has a similar ordering issue: it increments baseAmount before converting the token-denominated fee at the spot price. Router.addDividend then uses the resulting fee to calculate a reserve grant and calls Reserve.grantFunds followed by Pool.sync, permanently attributing the grant to existing LPs. The curated-pool check, rolling average, and dailyAllocation limit only constrain eligibility or the maximum allocation; they do not use an independent price, volume measure, or deviation bound and therefore do not prevent manipulation. Profitability depends on reserve size, liquidity, and LP ownership, but the exploitable valuation flaw is present.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Compute dividend eligibility from fee units and executed volume using a manipulation-resistant reference price, cap dividends per trade and per pool, and enforce pool-price deviation and minimum-liquidity limits before granting reserve funds.

medium

Router swaps and synth issuance lack user price protection against sandwich manipulation

This is a medium-severity transaction-integrity and missing-slippage-protection logic error affecting router swaps and synth issuance. The affected routes calculate execution results solely from the pool reserves at the time of execution and do not accept or enforce caller-specified minimum-output, maximum-input, price-limit, or deadline parameters. Cross-pool routes perform multiple mutable-price operations, increasing the opportunity for adverse execution.

An attacker can observe a pending swap or token-to-synth transaction, trade against the relevant pool to worsen its reserve ratio, allow the victim transaction to execute at the manipulated price, and then reverse the position after execution. The victim may receive fewer tokens or synths than expected, or spend more assets than intended. The absence of protection is especially consequential for routes involving two pool legs, where manipulation can affect either leg or both.

Affected Components: the Router contract and Pool contract, specifically buyTo(), sellTo(), swapTo(), swapAssetToSynth(), swapTo(), and mintSynth(), together with the user-facing router swap and synth-issuance entry points

QA Review: Upon reviewing Router.sol functions buyTo, sellTo, swapTo, swapAssetToSynth, and swapSynthToAsset, together with Pool.sol functions swapTo, mintSynth, and burnSynth, I confirmed that the alleged missing price protection exists and is exploitable. These routes calculate outputs from the pool’s current internal reserves through calcSwapOutput and immediately execute transfers, but expose no minimum-output, maximum-input, deadline, or caller-specified price-limit parameter. Pool.swapTo and mintSynth therefore accept whatever execution price results from reserves at inclusion time. An attacker can front-run a pending trade with an opposing pool transaction, worsen the reserve ratio, allow the victim transaction to execute at the manipulated price, and then trade back afterward. The cross-pool paths in Router.swapTo and swapSynthToAsset are particularly exposed because they perform sequential mutable-price legs without propagating any user tolerance. Although transaction atomicity and normal balance checks prevent outright theft or invalid transfers, they do not prevent unfavorable execution, and no slippage or freshness guard exists in the reviewed code.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Add minimum-output and maximum-input parameters, deadlines, and optional price limits to every swap and synth route; propagate those checks through both legs of cross-pool swaps and reject transactions outside the caller's tolerance.

    require(fromToken != toToken); // Tokens must not be the same
    if(fromToken == BASE){
        buyTo(inputAmount, toToken, member); // Swap SPARTA to TOKEN & tsf to user
    } else if(toToken == BASE) {
        sellTo(inputAmount, fromToken, member); // Swap TOKEN to SPARTA & tsf to user

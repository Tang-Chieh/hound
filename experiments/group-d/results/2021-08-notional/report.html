Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Notional lending protocol, reviewing its externally accessible actions, proxy and upgrade structure, governance controls, financial state transitions, valuation logic, and liquidation flows.

The review covered the high-level system architecture, including how users and integrations enter through ERC1967/UUPS proxies, routers, and action modules; how governance and pause controls manage protocol configuration; and how trading, settlement, collateral valuation, nTokens, markets, oracle interfaces, and liquidation components interact.

Our team confirmed a high-severity oracle freshness weakness in collateral valuation: ExchangeRate consumes Chainlink-style aggregator data while checking positivity but not freshness, round completeness, or maximum age. This can affect free-collateral checks and liquidation pricing when feeds become stale. Additional areas requiring remediation include single-source oracle reliance, asset-rate validation in fCash liquidation and settlement, and potential manipulation of market-derived values used by liquidations and nToken operations. The overall security posture is financially sensitive and should be strengthened with fail-closed oracle validation, deviation controls, and manipulation-resistant pricing safeguards.

System Overview

The Hound team analyzed Notional as a modular lending protocol exposed through nProxy, ERC1967Proxy, Proxy, and UUPSUpgradeable components. The proxy stack delegates user-facing calls to implementation logic while ERC1967Upgrade manages implementation changes and writes upgrade-related storage. StorageLayoutV1 is shared by governance and implementation paths, making authorization, initializer protection, storage compatibility, and upgrade governance important security boundaries.

Our analysis revealed that Router and PausedRouter direct calls into action modules such as TradingAction, AccountAction, BatchAction, GovernanceAction, liquidation actions, nToken actions, Views, and SettleAssetsExternal. GovernorAlpha authorizes governance operations, while PausedRouter preserves access to governance functionality during pauses. BatchAction and external integrations such as CompoundToNotionalV2 can combine settlement, trading, balance, and collateral operations, increasing the importance of atomic validation and correct account-context handling.

The core financial state is represented through AccountContext, account balances, AccountPortfolio bitmap assets, MarketParameters, and nToken ERC20 balances. BalanceHandler, PortfolioHandler, AccountContextHandler, TransferAssets, and nTokenHandler update these structures, while TradingAction and the liquidation modules use Market, CashGroup, FreeCollateral, ExchangeRate, and related valuation libraries. TokenHandler and Incentives additionally cross the boundary to underlying currency tokens and incentive transfers.

We discovered that valuation flows rely on external data sources at several security-sensitive points. ExchangeRate reads AggregatorV2V3Interface data for collateral valuation, while AssetRate reads AssetRateAdapter and CashMarketInterface data for asset conversion. These values feed free-collateral checks, liquidation calculations, fCash settlement, and other balance-affecting operations. The confirmed weakness is that positive oracle answers can remain usable without demonstrated freshness or round-validity checks, allowing stale prices to influence borrowing capacity and liquidation outcomes.

The Hound team also examined settlement and tokenized-liquidity paths. SettleAssetsExternal invokes SettlePortfolioAssets and SettleBitmapAssets, which can write both AccountBalances and AccountPortfolio after asset conversion. nToken minting and redemption interact with Market calculations and nToken balances, while LiquidateCurrency and LiquidatefCash update portfolios and collateral during permissionless liquidation. These paths should use independently validated, bounded, and manipulation-resistant rates, with conservative failure behavior, deviation monitoring, and transaction-level limits where market state can affect financial outcomes.

Stale oracle prices can be used to manipulate collateral valuation and liquidation outcomes

This oracle price manipulation vulnerability is caused by insufficient validation of prices returned by the external aggregator. The oracle integration appears to verify that the reported rate is positive, but it does not demonstrably enforce round completeness, round validity, update timestamps, or a bounded staleness policy. As a result, collateral valuation may continue using a price that no longer reflects current market conditions.

An attacker could wait for the feed to stop updating or become stale while the underlying collateral declines in value, then borrow against the overvalued collateral or withdraw and transfer assets using inflated free-collateral calculations. The same stale valuation could also distort liquidation calculations, allowing collateral to be acquired at an unfavorable price or causing liquidations to occur incorrectly. Because the affected valuation is used in borrowing, collateral checks, and liquidation-related operations, stale oracle data could result in significant losses to the protocol and its users.

Affected Components: the ExchangeRate and FreeCollateral libraries, and the AggregatorV2V3Interface oracle integration

QA Review: Upon reviewing ExchangeRate.buildExchangeRate in contracts/internal/valuation/ExchangeRate.sol, I confirmed that the oracle-consumption weakness is present. For non-ETH currencies, the function calls AggregatorV2V3Interface.latestRoundData() but discards the roundId, startedAt, updatedAt, and answeredInRound fields, validating only that the returned rate is greater than zero. No heartbeat, maximum-age, round-completeness, or answeredInRound consistency check is enforced. The resulting ETHRate is consumed by FreeCollateral.getFreeCollateralStateful, getFreeCollateralView, and getLiquidationFactors through _updateNetETHValue, and consequently affects checkFreeCollateralAndRevert, LiquidationHelpers.preLiquidationActions, and LiquidateCurrency liquidation pricing. Therefore, if the configured aggregator stops updating while its last positive answer is stale, the protocol continues valuing balances and calculating liquidation discounts and transfers using that obsolete price. The positivity check prevents zero or negative answers but does not mitigate stale data, so the hypothesis is not a false positive. Exploitability and impact depend on feed failure or halted updates and the configured oracle, but the missing freshness validation is clearly demonstrated.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Require a complete and successful oracle round, reject zero or negative answers, enforce updatedAt/max-age and heartbeat checks, and fail closed when the feed is stale or incomplete. Consider conservative fallback pricing and circuit breakers for abnormal deviations.

Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Lybra Protocol, focusing on its collateralized stablecoin, vault, rewards, governance, proxy, and cross-chain components.

Our review covered the system architecture, including user and liquidator interactions with collateral vaults, EUSD and PeUSD issuance and redemption flows, governance-controlled configuration, rewards distribution, oracle dependencies, proxy administration, and LayerZero-based cross-chain messaging.

The protocol has a modular architecture with centralized configuration and multiple external price dependencies. We confirmed a high-severity oracle-validation weakness in EUSDMiningIncentives: the esLBR buyout path trusts an owner-configured LBR feed without validating freshness, positivity, round completeness, deviation, or decimals, allowing rewards to be purchased at an improperly low EUSD price when the feed is stale, faulty, compromised, or misconfigured. We also identified a high-impact vault-oracle risk: collateral minting and liquidation calculations trust configured collateral prices and exchange ratios without visible validation safeguards. Overall, the security posture is materially dependent on the integrity and correctness of privileged oracle configuration and external pricing infrastructure.

System Overview

The Lybra Protocol is organized around LybraProxy, LybraProxyAdmin, and LybraConfigurator. The proxy routes calls to the configurator, while proxy administration and governance-controlled roles can manage implementation and protocol configuration. LybraConfigurator coordinates references to vaults, EUSD, PeUSD, rewards modules, stable assets, and external Curve-related components. GovernanceTimelock and DAO role holders provide privileged control over these settings, making authorization boundaries and configuration changes important security considerations.

Collateralized borrowing is implemented through LybraEUSDVaultBase and LybraPeUSDVaultBase. LybraStETHDepositVault extends the EUSD vault path, while LybraWstETHVault, LybraRETHVault, and LybraWBETHVault provide PeUSD collateral integrations. Users transfer collateral assets such as stETH, wstETH, rETH, and wbETH into the relevant vaults, after which the vaults use configured price feeds and collateral exchange-ratio sources to determine borrowing capacity. The vaults mint EUSD or PeUSD against collateral and later burn the corresponding stablecoin during repayment or liquidation. Liquidators can invoke liquidation paths that burn debt tokens and receive collateral transfers.

Our analysis revealed that vault valuation is a critical trust boundary. LybraEUSDVaultBase and LybraPeUSDVaultBase consume configured oracle results for mint health checks, withdrawal calculations, and liquidation debt-to-collateral settlement. The specialized vaults additionally rely on exchange-ratio functions such as stEthPerToken, getExchangeRatio, or exchangeRatio. The reviewed vault layer does not visibly enforce freshness, round completeness, positive values, deviation bounds, or independent-price consistency before using these results, so stale, manipulated, misconfigured, or otherwise invalid external pricing could lead to over-minting, wrongful liquidation, or incorrect collateral settlement.

The rewards subsystem includes EUSDMiningIncentives, ProtocolRewardsPool, StakingRewardsV2Pool, and esLBRBoost. EUSDMiningIncentives mints esLBR and uses an LBR price oracle when converting the bidding fee for purchaseOtherEarnings into EUSD. The confirmed weakness is that this conversion directly uses latestRoundData without validating the answer, round status, update time, or feed decimals, while hardcoding eight-decimal precision. A defective or compromised configured feed could therefore let a caller acquire another user’s esLBR rewards for substantially less than their intended value. Governance and timelock controls limit who can replace the feed, but they do not compensate for missing runtime validation.

PeUSD also implements the OFT cross-chain architecture through OFTCoreV2, IOFTV2, LzApp, NonblockingLzApp, and LayerZero endpoint interfaces. PeUSD transfers can send messages through the LayerZero messaging service to remote-chain OFT contracts, with receiver and nonblocking handling defining the cross-chain delivery path. This introduces additional security boundaries around trusted remote addresses, endpoint configuration, message validation, and failure recovery. The overall architecture is modular, but its safety depends on strict governance controls, validated oracle adapters, carefully configured collateral ratios, and robust cross-chain endpoint and peer management.

Unvalidated LBR Oracle Enables Underpriced EUSD Buyouts

This oracle price manipulation vulnerability arises because the EUSD buyout mechanism directly trusts an owner-configured LBR AggregatorV3 price feed without validating the feed’s freshness, round completeness, positivity, declared decimals, or deviation from an independent reference. The conversion logic also hardcodes an 8-decimal precision assumption, allowing incorrectly scaled prices to produce erroneous fee calculations.

An attacker who causes the configured LBR feed to return a stale, depressed, invalid, or incorrectly scaled price can invoke the purchaseOtherEarnings(bool) buyout path for a user with claimable rewards. The attacker may then pay an artificially low EUSD bidding fee while receiving the full esLBR reward, resulting in an underpriced acquisition and potential loss of value to the protocol or affected users. The risk is increased because the oracle can be changed through setLBROracle(address) without the described validation and activation safeguards.

Affected Components: the EUSDMiningIncentives contract, specifically the purchaseOtherEarnings(bool) and setLBROracle(address) functions, the LBR price oracle, and the EUSD token

QA Review: Upon reviewing EUSDMiningIncentives.purchaseOtherEarnings and setLBROracle, I confirmed an oracle-validation weakness with an exploitable underpricing path. purchaseOtherEarnings is publicly callable and, when useEUSD is true, obtains lbrPrice directly from lbrPriceFeed.latestRoundData(), casts it to uint256, and computes the EUSD fee as biddingFee * uint256(lbrPrice) / 1e8. The code performs no checks for answer positivity, round completeness, updatedAt freshness, or acceptable deviation, and it assumes the feed always uses eight decimals. Consequently, a zero or depressed positive answer can reduce the required EUSD payment substantially or to zero while the caller still receives the full esLBR reward. A feed using different decimals can also cause systematic mispricing. setLBROracle is restricted by onlyOwner, so an arbitrary external attacker cannot replace the feed directly; exploitation therefore depends on a faulty, compromised, stale, or improperly configured feed or privileged owner action. That authorization constraint limits exploitability but does not mitigate the missing validation in the buyout calculation. The same unchecked feeds and hardcoded precision also appear in stakedLBRLpValue.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Validate answer > 0, roundId and answeredInRound consistency, updatedAt freshness, and feed decimals before use; normalize using the feed's declared decimals rather than hardcoding 1e8. Add a maximum deviation bound against a trusted secondary source or TWAP and pause EUSD buyouts when the feed is stale or outside bounds. Restrict oracle changes behind timelocked governance and apply a delay before a newly configured feed is used.

Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Tokemak protocol, focusing on its core asset-management, vault, routing, oracle, statistics, rewards, and staking components.

The review covered the system architecture, including shared services such as SystemRegistry and AccessController; vault registries and factories; DestinationVault and LMPVault components; strategy and external-protocol adapters; swap routing; oracle providers; statistics calculators; and reward and staking integrations.

Our team found no confirmed exploitable vulnerabilities in the reviewed scope. The assessed security posture benefits from centralized role-based authorization, interface-driven component boundaries, registry-controlled deployment, validation in swap routing, and defensive validation in oracle handling. Invalid Chainlink responses, including negative answers, are rejected before unsigned conversion, although external integrations remain important operational dependencies that require ongoing configuration and monitoring.

System Overview

The Hound team identified SystemRegistry and AccessController as shared control-plane services. AccessController implements IAccessController and manages protocol roles, while dependent components such as DestinationVaultRegistry use the SystemRegistry to resolve system-wide dependencies. These services establish the authorization and configuration foundation for vault creation, registry updates, strategy operations, and other privileged actions.

DestinationVaultFactory creates and initializes DestinationVault instances and registers them with DestinationVaultRegistry through IDestinationVaultFactory. LMPVaultFactory similarly creates LMPVault instances and associated AbstractRewarder components, registering the resulting vaults with LMPVaultRegistry. Strategies interact with vaults through IStrategy, while destination integrations are abstracted behind IDestinationAdapter and related registry interfaces. This separation limits direct coupling between vault accounting and individual external pool implementations.

Our analysis revealed that asset deployment and rebalancing can reach external protocols through adapters such as CurveV2FactoryCryptoAdapter, MaverickAdapter, and VelodromeStakingAdapter. DestinationVault transfers external ERC-20 and pool tokens, while reward adapters claim incentives from external gauges and reward systems. SwapRouter implements ISwapRouter, validates synchronous swapper integrations through ISyncSwapper, and routes conversions through CurveV2Swap, UniV3Swap, and external AMM pools. These boundaries concentrate external-call and token-flow risk in identifiable adapter and routing components.

Oracle and valuation functionality is provided through IPriceOracle-compatible components, including ChainlinkOracle, EthPeggedOracle, WstETHEthOracle, and BalancerLPMetaStableEthOracle. WstETHEthOracle reads from IRootPriceOracle, while BalancerLPMetaStableEthOracle reads pricing data from external AMM infrastructure. ChainlinkOracle validates provider data, including requiring a positive answer and checking round, timestamp, and freshness conditions before normalization, reducing the risk of signed-answer conversion errors.

StatsCalculatorFactory creates implementations such as CurveV1ConvexStatsCalculator, which computes pool statistics, while incentive pricing and AbstractRewarder components calculate reward-related values using token and oracle data. The Hound team identified these valuation and reward paths as security-sensitive because they combine external data, token balances, pool state, and privileged configuration. Our analysis found no confirmed issue in the reviewed architecture, but safe operation depends on maintaining correct registry entries, trusted adapter deployments, sound oracle configuration, and appropriate access-control administration.

Findings

No confirmed vulnerabilities were identified during this audit.

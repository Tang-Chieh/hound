Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Telcoin Staking and FeeBuyback Protocol, with emphasis on the interactions between staking users, administrative roles, reward plugins, token custody, and external services.

The review covered the system architecture, including user-facing staking and claiming flows, the StakingModule and its pluggable IPlugin reward contracts, TEL and other ERC-20 token transfers, SimplePlugin reward funding, FeeBuyback swap execution, ownership controls, recovery mechanisms, pausing, and trust boundaries involving external wallets and aggregators.

Our team confirmed a high-severity economic risk in FeeBuyback.submit: the contract forwards caller-supplied aggregator calldata and checks only low-level call success, without enforcing minimum TEL output, execution-price limits, deadlines, or a swap-specific output balance delta. This can permit unfavorable execution and sandwich-style value extraction, while the referral credit may include TEL retained from earlier operations. The staking architecture includes meaningful role-based controls and checkpointed accounting, but the buyback flow requires stronger execution validation before it can be considered robust against market manipulation.

System Overview

The Hound team identified StakingModule as the primary user-facing staking contract. Users can stake TEL, exit, claim rewards, perform combined claim-and-exit operations, and query balances, total supply, and claimable amounts. StakingModule maintains stake checkpoints and aggregate staking state, transfers TEL during stake and exit operations, and initializes the TEL token address and plugin registry. Administrative functionality includes plugin management, pausing, slashing, token rescue, and migration-oriented stake and exit operations.

StakingModule integrates with pluggable reward contracts through the IPlugin interface. SimplePlugin is the principal reward implementation represented in the architecture: it maintains checkpointed claimable balances and total amounts owed, exposes claim and view functions, and receives stake-change notifications from StakingModule. During staking, exits, or other balance changes, StakingModule notifies registered plugins so reward accounting can remain synchronized with user stake history. SimplePlugin pays claims in TEL and uses an increaser address to control reward funding.

The reward funding path crosses from FeeBuyback into SimplePlugin through the ISimplePlugin.increaseClaimableBy interface. FeeBuyback can receive assets from the configured safe, interact with an external swap aggregator, approve token spending, and transfer resulting TEL to the plugin while increasing claimable rewards for a referral recipient. This creates a trust boundary around both the configured aggregator and the submitted swap calldata. Our analysis revealed that FeeBuyback.submit does not enforce a minimum TEL amount, maximum execution price, deadline, or positive balance delta attributable to the current swap; it relies only on the external call succeeding.

We discovered that FeeBuyback subsequently credits the referral recipient using the contract's full TEL balance rather than isolating the output generated by the current transaction. Combined with unconstrained aggregator execution, this can expose safe-held funds to adverse pricing and may cause previously retained TEL to be included in a new referral credit. The contract also includes an ERC-20 rescue path subject to its configured executor controls, while ownership and executor administration are managed through TieredOwnership.

Security controls are distributed across role-based access restrictions and ownership mechanisms. StakingModule uses SLASHER_ROLE, PLUGIN_EDITOR_ROLE, PAUSER_ROLE, and RECOVERY_ROLE to separate slashing, plugin administration, emergency pausing, rescue, and migration capabilities. FeeBuyback relies on owner and executor controls, but external swap execution remains dependent on the correctness and protections of supplied aggregator routes. Strengthening contract-level slippage, deadline, route, and balance-delta validation is therefore necessary to reduce the principal identified economic risk.

FeeBuyback swap is exposed to sandwich and price manipulation due to missing slippage enforcement

This is a logic error in the FeeBuyback swap validation: the buyback flow considers an aggregator call successful solely when the low-level call returns successfully, without validating the amount of TEL received or the execution price. The submit() function forwards caller-supplied swapData and the transaction value to the aggregator, but does not enforce a minimum output amount, deadline, route constraints, or an acceptable price bound. It subsequently credits the referral mechanism using the contract's TEL balance rather than verifying the positive balance increase attributable to the current swap.

An attacker can front-run a buyback submission by trading against the relevant liquidity pool or route to manipulate its price, allowing the buyback to execute at an unfavorable rate with the unconstrained swapData. The attacker can then reverse the trade and capture the resulting price difference, while the safe receives fewer TEL rewards. This can cause repeated economic loss whenever an attacker can anticipate or influence buyback execution. The flow should require and enforce a minimum TEL output, validate a deadline and approved route parameters, and credit only the positive TEL balance delta generated by the current swap.

Affected Components: the fee submission entry point, the FeeBuyback contract, specifically the submit() function, and the aggregator service

QA Review: After analyzing FeeBuyback.submit in contracts/fee-buyback/FeeBuyback.sol, I confirmed that the buyback execution lacks contract-level slippage and output validation. For non-TEL tokens, the function transfers exactly amount from _safe, approves _aggregator, and then executes arbitrary swapData through _aggregator.call{value: msg.value}(swapData). The only check is the low-level boolean swapResult, which confirms call success but does not verify the amount of TEL received, the realized exchange rate, a deadline, or a minimum output. Consequently, if the supplied aggregator calldata permits unconstrained execution, a market participant can front-run the owner transaction, manipulate the relevant liquidity, allow the swap to execute at an unfavorable price, and reverse the position afterward. The onlyOwner modifier restricts who submits the transaction but does not prevent public mempool sandwiching. Additionally, the subsequent referral credit uses _telcoin.balanceOf(address(this)) rather than the balance delta generated by this swap, potentially crediting prior retained TEL as well. Aggregator-side protections may reduce practical exploitability for particular routes, but they are not enforced by this contract.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Require and enforce a minimum TEL output derived from an off-chain quote or trusted oracle, include a deadline and route-specific validation, and credit only the positive TEL balance delta produced by the current swap. Prefer aggregator calldata that embeds a checked minimum return amount and reject execution when the realized price is outside approved bounds.

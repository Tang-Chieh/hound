Executive Summary

The Hound team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Malt Protocol, reviewing its core token and transfer-verification controls, bonding and reward mechanisms, auction and stabilization processes, liquidity and collateral services, market-data flows, and external ERC-20, Uniswap, and governance interfaces.

Our review covered the high-level system architecture, including how contracts interact across token transfers, pool verification, bonding, mining, reward distribution, auction operations, stabilization, liquidity management, collateral accounting, time-series data, decentralized exchange integrations, and governance administration.

The system contains multiple access-control, verification, and modular interface boundaries, but we identified a confirmed high-severity price-manipulation risk in reward reinvestment. RewardReinvestor routes rewards through UniswapHandler.buyMalt(), which performs a swap with zero minimum output protection, allowing a pending reinvestment transaction to be sandwiched and executed at a materially unfavorable rate. The overall security posture therefore requires remediation of slippage and price-integrity controls before relying on the reinvestment flow for value-sensitive operations.

System Overview

The Hound team identified Malt as the principal protocol token contract implementing IMalt and depending on TransferService for transfer-policy enforcement. TransferService checks sender and recipient verifiers through ITransferVerification implementations, including PoolTransferVerification, which reads protocol data from IMaltDataLab. This creates a transfer trust boundary around token movements and allows administrative management of verifier configuration.

Bonding and rewards are organized around Bonding, MiningService, AbstractRewardMine, ERC20VestedMine, and RewardDistributor. Bonding handles stake or liquidity-token interactions and calls MiningService, while vested mines reference bonding and distribution state. RewardDistributor allocates rewards to reward mines and can route amounts to ForfeitHandler. RewardThrottle adds distribution-rate controls using DAO and bonding information and can direct excess rewards to RewardOverflowPool, which manages reward-token transfers. RewardReinvestor converts withdrawn rewards through the exchange layer before returning value to the bonding and mining flows.

Auction and stabilization components coordinate monetary-policy actions. Auction uses MaltDataLab for market information, invokes IAuctionStartController, and consults AuctionBurnReserveSkew for auction-specific burn or reserve adjustments. AuctionPool and AuctionParticipant support participation and auction accounting. StabilizerNode orchestrates auction operations with IDexHandler, IRewardThrottle, ILiquidityExtension, IImpliedCollateralService, and ISwingTrader, and it can mint Malt and transfer reward tokens as part of stabilization actions. ImpliedCollateralService further coordinates AuctionPool, RewardOverflowPool, LiquidityExtension, and SwingTrader.

Market data and liquidity services rely on Uniswap pool reserves, MaltDataLab, MovingAverage, UniswapHandler, and UniswapV2Library. MaltDataLab records reserve-derived information and writes to MovingAverage, while LiquidityExtension and UniswapHandler consume reserve data for liquidity and trading operations. Our analysis revealed that UniswapHandler.buyMalt() swaps the complete reward balance with amountOutMin set to zero, and RewardReinvestor.splitReinvest() does not accept or propagate a user-defined minimum Malt output or meaningful execution bound. This permits reserve manipulation around a pending reinvestment transaction, causing the swap to succeed at an unfavorable price; the subsequent bonding path does not provide a reliable economic safeguard against the loss.

Governance is represented by DAO and Timelock, with Timelock authorizing DAO-controlled administration. The architecture also includes interface boundaries for core services such as bonding, mining, auctions, liquidity, collateral, rewards, forfeiture, and exchange handling, which support modular deployment but increase the importance of validating implementation addresses and privileged configuration changes. Recommended remediation includes propagating caller-specified minimum-output and deadline parameters into reinvestment swaps, applying robust oracle or TWAP-based validation where appropriate, and reviewing the analogous zero-output protection in sellMalt().

Reward reinvestment is vulnerable to spot-price sandwich manipulation

This price-manipulation vulnerability results from the reward reinvestment flow lacking a user-supplied slippage limit and the Uniswap swap being executed with amountOutMin set to zero. After withdrawing rewards, RewardReinvestor delegates their conversion to Malt to UniswapHandler.buyMalt(), which swaps the entire reward balance using instantaneous Uniswap reserves without enforcing a minimum acceptable output or transaction deadline. Because these reserves can be temporarily influenced through large trades or flash-loan-funded transactions, the swap is permitted to execute at an unfavorable price as long as it returns a nonzero amount of Malt.

An attacker can monitor pending reinvestment transactions, manipulate the reward/Malt pool price before the transaction executes, and cause the protected transaction to receive materially less Malt than expected. The attacker can then reverse the temporary price movement and retain the extracted value. This can reduce user returns and, depending on the subsequent bonding logic, may cause the reinvested position to be created with substantially less value. The flow should require a caller-provided minimum Malt output and deadline, propagate these constraints to the router, and use a manipulation-resistant TWAP or oracle-derived bound for system-level validation rather than relying solely on current pool reserves.

Affected Components: the RewardReinvestor and UniswapHandler contracts, specifically the reward withdrawal and reinvestment flow and the buyMalt() function, together with the Uniswap pool reserve data source used for pricing

QA Review: Upon reviewing RewardReinvestor.splitReinvest() in src/contracts/RewardReinvestor.sol and UniswapHandler.buyMalt() in src/contracts/DexHandlers/UniswapHandler.sol, I confirmed the alleged spot-price manipulation vulnerability. splitReinvest() withdraws rewards, transfers half to the handler, and invokes buyMalt() without accepting or propagating any user-supplied minimum Malt output or deadline. buyMalt() computes the complete reward balance and calls router.swapExactTokensForTokens() with amountOutMin set to 0 and deadline set only to now. Consequently, an attacker can front-run a pending reinvestment transaction by moving the reward/Malt Uniswap reserves, cause the swap to execute at an unfavorable but non-reverting rate, and reverse the reserve manipulation afterward. The subsequent _bondAccount() flow does not validate the purchased Malt amount against an expected price; its addLiquidity() call only applies a 95% balance-based minimum and is not a reliable protection against a modest sandwich. The same zero-output protection also exists in sellMalt(), although it is not required for this attack. No relevant oracle or TWAP guard is used in the reinvestment path.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Require the reinvestment caller to supply a minimum acceptable Malt output and deadline, propagate that bound into the router swap, and revert when it is not met. Prefer a manipulation-resistant TWAP or oracle-derived bound for any system-level validation, and avoid calculating protection solely from the current pool reserves.

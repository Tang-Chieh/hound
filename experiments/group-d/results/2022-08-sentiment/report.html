Executive Summary

The Hound team conducted this comprehensive audit of the protocol architecture, focusing on the components that govern account creation, collateral management, oracle pricing, lending, external protocol interactions, and upgradeability.

The review covered the high-level system architecture and end-to-end control and data flows among users, AccountManager, AccountFactory, BeaconProxy accounts, Registry, RiskEngine, OracleFacade, LToken markets, protocol controllers, oracle adapters, and deployment components.

Our team confirmed a high-severity stale-price risk in ChainlinkOracle: the implementation accepts feed answers without validating freshness, round integrity, or zero values. This can affect collateral valuation, borrowing authorization, and liquidation decisions. Additional risks involving privileged oracle replacement, ERC4626 share pricing, and Yearn share pricing require stronger controls or implementation-specific validation. The reviewed LP valuation concern was not established as an exploitable spot-price manipulation issue based on the available implementations. Overall, the architecture has meaningful separation of responsibilities and protocol-specific validation, but oracle integrity and administrative configuration controls require priority remediation.

System Overview

The Hound team identified AccountManager as the primary account lifecycle and control component. AccountFactory creates BeaconProxy account instances, which route calls through Beacon to the Account implementation. AccountManager initializes accounts, invokes account operations, and coordinates risk validation before sensitive actions. Beacon-based routing provides upgradeability, but it also makes Beacon administration and implementation changes critical security boundaries.

Our analysis revealed that Account.exec can interact with external DeFi protocols through ControllerFacade and protocol-specific controllers. Controllers implementing IController validate supported interactions for protocols including Uniswap V2 and V3, Aave V2 and V3, Compound, ERC4626 vaults, Balancer, Curve, Yearn, WETH, and related integrations. This design limits arbitrary account interactions by placing protocol-specific validation between account execution and external targets.

Registry, RiskEngine, OracleFacade, and LToken form the primary financial control path. RiskEngine reads market configuration from Registry, obtains asset prices through OracleFacade, inspects account and lending positions, and evaluates collateral and debt health. LToken markets use DefaultRateModel for interest calculations and interact with external protocol state where required. Because borrowing and liquidation decisions depend on RiskEngine valuations, the correctness, freshness, and administrative integrity of oracle data are central to system solvency.

OracleFacade routes pricing requests to adapters including ChainlinkOracle, WETHOracle, ATokenOracle, ERC4626Oracle, YTokenOracle, Stable2CurveOracle, UniV2LPOracle, and WeightedBalancerLPOracle. The confirmed weakness is in ChainlinkOracle, which discards latestRoundData metadata and checks only for negative answers, leaving stale, incomplete, or zero feed responses available to downstream valuation. ERC4626 and Yearn adapters also warrant bounded exchange-rate, freshness, and redeemability controls because their values may depend on mutable vault accounting.

Deployment scripts wire the Registry, account components, risk controls, controllers, oracle adapters, and lending markets together. The architecture therefore relies heavily on correct deployment configuration and privileged administration of oracle mappings and protocol registrations. Recommended hardening includes enforcing Chainlink timestamp and round checks, validating oracle replacements through controlled governance and delayed activation, monitoring configuration changes, and applying conservative collateral limits to mutable vault-based assets.

Stale Chainlink rounds can remain trusted for collateral valuation

This is an oracle-validation logic error in which the Chainlink adapter appears to accept feed responses based only on whether the reported answer is non-negative. It does not enforce essential freshness and round-integrity conditions, such as a nonzero update timestamp, a valid round identifier, a consistent answeredInRound value, or a configured maximum age for the feed. As a result, the OracleFacade can route stale data to the protocol's collateral valuation and risk checks without detecting that the external price is no longer current.

An attacker could wait for the underlying market price to move while the Chainlink feed is stale or temporarily unavailable, then deposit collateral or borrow assets using an outdated and favorable valuation. This could cause the RiskEngine to approve undercollateralized positions, delay liquidations, or otherwise miscalculate account health, potentially resulting in protocol losses. The adapter should require valid round metadata and enforce per-feed freshness limits, reverting or suspending valuation when these checks fail; deviation limits and carefully designed fallback mechanisms may provide additional protection.

Affected Components: the ChainlinkOracle adapter, the Chainlink feed integration, and the OracleFacade price-routing component

QA Review: Upon reviewing the ChainlinkOracle implementation in oracle/src/chainlink/ChainlinkOracle.sol, I confirmed that price data is accepted without freshness or round-integrity validation. In getPrice(), the contract destructures latestRoundData() while discarding roundId, updatedAt, and answeredInRound, then rejects only answers below zero before converting the result against getEthPrice(). The getEthPrice() function applies the same limited negative-answer check to the ETH/USD feed and likewise ignores all metadata. There is no updatedAt != 0 requirement, maximum-age or heartbeat enforcement, answeredInRound >= roundId validation, or protection against a zero answer. OracleFacade forwards the configured oracle price directly, and RiskEngine._valueInWei() uses that price for all collateral and debt health calculations, so a stale favorable feed can authorize borrowing or delay liquidation. The deployment uses ArbiChainlinkOracle, whose implementation is not included; however, unless it overrides getPrice() with equivalent freshness checks, it inherits this vulnerable logic. The shown code therefore establishes a clear exploitable stale-price path.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Require positive answer, valid round metadata, updatedAt greater than zero, answeredInRound at least roundId, and a configured per-feed maximum age. Revert or suspend valuation when freshness checks fail, and add deviation or fallback controls where appropriate.

Executive Summary

The Hound security team conducted this comprehensive audit of the Sandclock protocol, including its vault, strategy, factory, depositor, claimer, and external-protocol integrations.

The review covered the high-level system architecture, component contracts, trust boundaries, user deposit and withdrawal flows, yield claiming, vault-to-strategy investment and redemption, asynchronous EthAnchor interactions, Curve conversions for non-UST assets, integration callbacks, and factory-controlled deployment.

Our team confirmed two high-severity price-manipulation risks. NonUSTStrategy relies on manipulable Curve spot quotations for vault share valuation and performs Curve swaps with a zero minimum output, allowing distorted pool conditions to cause excess share issuance or materially unfavorable asset conversion. The overall security posture is therefore high risk for vaults using NonUSTStrategy until valuation and swap-slippage protections are added.

System Overview

Sandclock is organized around Vault, Depositors, Claimers, strategy contracts, and SandclockFactory. Vault manages user deposits, claims, withdrawals, sponsorship, share accounting, and strategy investment. Depositors and Claimers maintain deposit and yield-related state, while integration detection uses ERC165Query to identify optional IIntegration callbacks during deposit and withdrawal processing. SandclockFactory exposes deployment paths for vaults and DCA instances through trusted factory-controlled functions.

User funds enter through Vault.deposit, which records deposit state, transfers the underlying asset into the vault, and creates the associated deposit and claim records. Vault.claimYield and the withdrawal paths coordinate with Claimers and Depositors before transferring assets to users. Vault._createClaim and Vault._withdrawDeposit calculate share-related amounts using total underlying value, total shares, sponsorship adjustments, and PercentMath arithmetic. Vault.setStrategy and Vault.updateInvested control the relationship between the vault and its configured IStrategy implementation.

BaseStrategy provides the common investment lifecycle for UST and non-UST strategies. It coordinates deposits into and redemptions from EthAnchor through asynchronous doHardWork, finishDepositStable, initRedeemStable, and finishRedeemStable operations. Withdrawals to the vault can initiate redemption and transfer recovered assets back to Vault. Invested-asset calculations also depend on the exchange-rate feeder, making external pricing and callback behavior important trust-boundary considerations.

NonUSTStrategy extends BaseStrategy by converting between the configured underlying asset and UST through Curve. Our analysis revealed that its investedAssets valuation uses Curve.get_dy_underlying as a live spot quote without an observed TWAP, deviation limit, freshness check, or liquidity-bound validation. The Hound team identified this as a confirmed high-severity issue because a temporary Curve price distortion can affect Vault share issuance and withdrawal accounting. This finding was confirmed by openai-codex/gpt-5.6-luna.

We discovered a second confirmed high-severity issue in NonUSTStrategy swap settlement: Curve exchange_underlying is called with min_dy set to zero for both conversion directions. In particular, the permissionless redemption completion path can settle while Curve is temporarily manipulated, accepting an arbitrarily poor non-reverting output. This issue was also confirmed by openai-codex/gpt-5.6-luna. Recommended protections include manipulation-resistant valuation, oracle and pool-liquidity bounds, explicit deviation checks, and conservative transaction-level minimum-output parameters.

Manipulable Curve spot quote directly controls NonUST share pricing

This price-manipulation vulnerability results from the vault treating a directly queried Curve spot quote as an authoritative valuation of NonUST assets. The NonUSTStrategy.investedAssets function derives the asset value from Curve.get_dy_underlying, and Vault.totalUnderlying uses that value when calculating shares for deposits and withdrawals. The implementation does not apply a time-weighted or independently validated price, freshness checks, deviation limits, or minimum-liquidity requirements.

An attacker can use flash liquidity or a large temporary trade to depress the Curve quote, then deposit assets while the valuation is artificially low. Because the vault calculates shares using the manipulated value, the attacker can receive an excessive number of shares. After the Curve price is restored, those shares represent a disproportionately large claim on the vault’s assets, allowing the attacker to withdraw more than was legitimately deposited and potentially dilute other depositors.

Affected Components: the NonUSTStrategy contract, specifically the investedAssets() function, and the Vault contract, specifically the totalUnderlying(), deposit(), createDeposit(), withdrawDeposit(), and withdraw() functions

QA Review: Upon reviewing Vault.totalUnderlying, _createDeposit, _createClaim, _withdrawDeposit, and NonUSTStrategy.investedAssets, I confirmed the price-manipulation vulnerability. NonUSTStrategy.investedAssets values the strategy’s UST-denominated assets using curvePool.get_dy_underlying(ustI, underlyingI, ustAssets), which is a live Curve spot quote with no TWAP, deviation, freshness, liquidity, or intra-transaction consistency check. Vault.totalUnderlying incorporates this quote directly, and _createClaim computes shares as amount * totalShares / totalUnderlyingMinusSponsored before the deposit transfer occurs. A temporary reduction in the Curve quote therefore lowers the denominator and mints excess shares to a depositor. After the pool is restored, _withdrawDeposit recalculates the shares attributable to the recorded principal using the restored valuation, potentially burning fewer shares than were minted; the residual shares remain associated with the claimer and can be realized through yieldFor and claimYield, subject to available vault liquidity and the configured lock period. No code-level guard prevents this sequence, so the hypothesis is exploitable where the vault has existing shares and sufficient liquidity.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Use a manipulation-resistant valuation such as a bounded TWAP or independently verified oracle, enforce deviation and liquidity checks, and avoid allowing deposits or withdrawals when the valuation is outside configured bounds or changes abnormally within a transaction.

Zero-output protection allows Curve price manipulation to steal strategy assets during swaps

This price-manipulation vulnerability is caused by strategy swaps accepting any Curve output amount because both swap paths hard-code the minimum output parameter to zero. NonUSTStrategy._swapUnderlyingToUst and _swapUstToUnderlying therefore execute at whatever exchange rate the pool provides, without comparing the result with a trusted quote, enforcing a slippage limit, or checking for excessive price impact. The redeem-side settlement path can also be initiated by an arbitrary caller through finishRedeemStable().

An attacker can temporarily distort Curve’s reserves using a large trade or flash liquidity, then trigger finishRedeemStable while the pool is manipulated. The strategy will execute the zero-minimum-output swap at the unfavorable rate, potentially converting strategy assets for substantially less value than expected. The attacker can then restore the pool state, while the resulting loss remains with the strategy and its users. This issue can directly cause asset theft or material value extraction rather than merely affecting an informational price.

Affected Components: the NonUSTStrategy contract, specifically the _swapUnderlyingToUst(), _swapUstToUnderlying(), and finishRedeemStable() functions, and the Curve interface used for exchange_underlying()

QA Review: Upon reviewing NonUSTStrategy._swapUnderlyingToUst, _swapUstToUnderlying, and finishRedeemStable, I confirmed the stated price-manipulation vulnerability. Both swap paths call curvePool.exchange_underlying with min_dy set to zero, so Curve provides no execution-price protection: any non-reverting output amount is accepted. The redeem path is especially exploitable because BaseStrategy.finishRedeemStable is public and NonUSTStrategy.finishRedeemStable invokes _swapUstToUnderlying immediately after receiving UST, allowing any caller to trigger settlement while the pool is temporarily distorted. Although doHardWork is restricted, that restriction does not protect the redeem-side conversion. No trusted quote, oracle comparison, maximum price impact, liquidity check, or caller-supplied slippage bound is used before either swap. The investedAssets view uses get_dy_underlying, but this is only an informational valuation and does not constrain exchange_underlying execution. Consequently, an attacker able to manipulate the Curve pool around settlement can cause strategy UST or underlying assets to be converted at an unfavorable rate; the zero minimum output permits the loss to execute rather than reverting.
Suggested Mitigation (not independently verified): Compute a trusted expected output immediately before the swap and pass a conservative min_dy with an explicit slippage limit. Add price-impact, pool-liquidity, and oracle-deviation checks, and restrict or otherwise safely coordinate permissionless settlement so a caller cannot settle during an observable manipulated state.
